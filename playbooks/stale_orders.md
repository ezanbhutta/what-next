# Stale open orders

An order is stale once it has been open more than 60 days. Past that point it
is not late, it is forgotten: nobody is chasing it, the designer has moved on,
and the buyer has stopped expecting anything.

**Owner: Ezan.** Not the CSR on shift, not the designer on the row. One person
holds the whole list, because the common failure is each order looking like
somebody else's.

This file is instructions, not a script. There is no message to copy — the
right message depends entirely on which of the three buckets the order is in,
and picking the bucket is the actual work.

## The only question that matters

For each order, decide which of three things is true:

1. **We owe work.** The buyer is waiting on us. This is the expensive one —
   it is a live delivery failure and it is also the one most likely to end in
   a refund or a public 1-star.
2. **They owe a reply.** We delivered or sent a revision; the buyer went quiet.
3. **It is dead.** Nothing will happen. The buyer is gone.

Do not skip this step and start writing messages. An order in bucket 1 that
gets a "just checking in" message becomes a dispute, because the buyer reads
it as being asked to chase us for work they paid for.

## Bucket 1 — we owe work

- Assign it to the designer named on the row. If that person has left or is
  on another project, reassign it now rather than noting it.
- Give a specific delivery date this week. Not "shortly", not "in a few days".
- Message the buyer with the date before the work starts, not after it lands.
  A date that arrives before the file does is what rebuilds trust.
- If the order is old enough that the brief has gone stale, ask one question
  to confirm the brief still stands — but only one, and only after the date.

## Bucket 2 — they owe a reply

- One message. State plainly what was last delivered and when.
- Ask a single closed question: is this still live, yes or no.
- Do not re-open the brief, do not offer new concepts, do not discount. Any
  of those reads as desperation and invites a renegotiation you did not need
  to have.
- If there is no reply within a week, it moves to bucket 3.

## Bucket 3 — it is dead

- Set the status so the order stops appearing on this list. An order that
  keeps resurfacing after it has been judged dead trains everyone to ignore
  the list.
- Write why in the CSR column, in one line. The next person needs to know
  whether this buyer left angry or simply vanished — those are different
  clients if they ever come back.
- Do not send a farewell message. There is nothing to gain and a dormant
  buyer who gets a "we're closing this" note sometimes leaves a review on
  the way out.

## What not to do

- **Never ask about a review, or mention ratings, in any of these messages.**
  These are late orders. A review request attached to a late delivery is the
  single most reliable way to convert a private 3-star into a public one, and
  soliciting in exchange for anything breaches Fiverr's terms outright.
- Do not bulk-send. Six tailored messages beat twenty identical ones, and the
  identical ones are visible to buyers who talk to each other.
- Do not reopen an order the buyer has already accepted, to "check they're
  happy". That reopens the revision window on work that is closed.

## When it is working

The 60+ band shrinks week on week and the 31–60 band does not grow to
replace it. If the old band empties while the middle band fills, orders are
not being closed — they are being aged into a different row of the same
table.
