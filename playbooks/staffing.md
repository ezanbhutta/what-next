# Playbook — Staffing and routing

## CSR conversion (leads ≥ 20)

| CSR | Leads | Placed | Conversion | Value/lead |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Salman | 40 | 14 | 35.0% | $53 |
| Ezan | 125 | 31 | 24.8% | $16 |
| Tanzeel | 179 | 40 | 22.3% | $7 |
| Tayyab | 38 | 8 | 21.1% | **$100** |

Read the last two columns together. Tayyab has the *lowest* conversion but by far the
*highest* value per lead — he closes fewer deals and much bigger ones. Ranking him on
conversion alone would be exactly the wrong call.

**Route accordingly:** Tayyab and Salman get the high-value and Tier 1 leads. Volume
routing to Ezan and Tanzeel.

The daily brief ranks on the Wilson lower bound, not the raw rate, so a lucky small sample
cannot jump the queue.

| Designer | Orders | AOV |
|---|---|---|
| Amin | 21 | $195 |

| Dulal Khan | 24 | $156 |
| Hamid | 33 | $148 |
| Bilal | 23 | $144 |
| Zohaib | 17 | $105 |

**Before acting on this, check the direction of causation.** A designer may have a low AOV
because they are handed cheap briefs, not because they produce cheap outcomes. Compare the
brief mix before concluding anything about skill.

If the mix is comparable, route briefs above $200 to Amin and Musharaf. High-value briefs
carry the most dispute risk *and* the most upsell potential, so they belong with the people
who already deliver at that level.

Note: "Provided by Client" appears in the designer column with the highest apparent AOV.
It is a placeholder meaning the buyer supplied the artwork, not a person. The engine
excludes it.
