# Playbook — Dispute / refund rescue

**Trigger:** the engine detects refund, dispute, cancel or chargeback language on a live order.

## Why this outranks everything else

A cancelled order on Fiverr does not just cost the order value. It hits order-completion
rate, which feeds the gig's success score, which feeds impressions. The visible star
rating barely moves — with 1,582 reviews a single 1-star shifts the average by about
0.002 — so **do not reassure yourself with the rating math**. The damage is in the
success score and in the private feedback the buyer leaves, which you never see.

The order at risk today (Dr. Ali Albalawi, #5) has raised refunds more than once and has
still chosen more revisions each time. That is a buyer who wants to be rescued, not one
who wants their money back. Treat it that way.

## The sequence

1. **Read everything first.** Every prior concept, every meeting note. Re-showing a
   rejected direction is the single fastest way to convert "frustrated" into "refund".
2. **Reply within 2 hours.** Response speed is itself a success-score input, and silence
   reads as avoidance.
3. **Acknowledge in their words.** "You've been clear that the concepts feel closed and
   heavy rather than open and flowing" beats "sorry you're unhappy". Never defend the work.
4. **Give exactly two doors, both good for them:**
   - *Door A* — one named senior designer takes a genuinely fresh direction, fixed date,
     no extra cost.
   - *Door B* — clean partial refund, mutual cancellation, no review either way.
5. **Do not offer unlimited revisions.** That is how #5 reached this state: many rounds,
   no convergence. More rounds with the same process produce the same result.
6. **If they pick A, change something structural** — a different designer, a different
   creative lead, a 20-minute call before any design work. Same inputs, same output.
7. **If they pick B, process it the same day.** A fast clean exit costs less than a slow
   one, and buyers who exit cleanly sometimes come back.

## Escalate to CEO immediately when

- The buyer opens a Fiverr Resolution Center dispute.
- The order value is above $300.
- The buyer mentions reporting the account, or "reporting" appears anywhere.

## Never

- Never ask a buyer to withdraw a review in exchange for anything. That is a ToS
  violation and risks the whole account, which is worth vastly more than any order.
- Never argue the revision count. You will win the argument and lose the account health.
